Before any interpretation, Vedic astrology is a coordinate system.
Every point in the 360-degree zodiac belongs to exactly one of 12
**രാശികൾ** (rashis, 30 degrees each), one of 27 **നക്ഷത്രങ്ങൾ**
(nakshatras, 13 degrees 20 minutes each), and one of that nakshatra's
4 **പാദങ്ങൾ** (padas, 3 degrees 20 minutes each) -- no ambiguity, no
interpretation required, just arithmetic on a longitude. This page is
that layer only: what's *there*, not what it *means*.

Why Most Nakshatras Straddle a Rashi Boundary
--------------------------------------------------------

രാശികളും നക്ഷത്രങ്ങളും don't share a common boundary size -- 30
degrees doesn't divide evenly by 13 degrees 20 minutes -- so most
നക്ഷത്രങ്ങൾ straddle a രാശി boundary somewhere inside them. What *is*
exact: 4 പാദങ്ങൾ of 3 degrees 20 minutes each always equal one
നക്ഷത്രം, and 9 പാദങ്ങൾ always equal one രാശി (30 divided by 3 degrees
20 minutes is exactly 9) -- which is exactly why padas, not degrees,
are the unit later techniques like Navamsa are built on.

അട്ട (Atta): The Three Ways a Nakshatra Splits
--------------------------------------------------------

Exactly 9 of the 27 nakshatras straddle a rashi boundary; the other
18 fit entirely inside one rashi. Those 9 split nakshatras fall into
three named patterns, and which pattern a given nakshatra gets is not
arbitrary -- it's fixed by that nakshatra's position in the 9-planet
lord cycle, the same cycle Vimshottari Dasha is built on:

.. list-table::
   :class: longtable
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 20 15 35 30

   * - Malayalam term
     - Split
     - Nakshatras
     - Shared lord
   * - തലയറ്റത് (Thalayattathu / Headless)
     - 1 : 3
     - കാർത്തിക (Krittika), ഉത്രം (Uttara Phalguni), ഉത്രാടം (Uttara Ashadha)
     - സൂര്യൻ (Sun)
   * - ഉടലറ്റത് (Udalattathu / Body severed)
     - 2 : 2
     - മകയിരം (Mrigashira), ചിത്തിര (Chitra), അവിട്ടം (Dhanishta)
     - ചൊവ്വ (Mars)
   * - കാലറ്റത് (Kaalattathu / Leg severed)
     - 3 : 1
     - പുണർതം (Punarvasu), വിശാഖം (Vishakha), പൂരുരുട്ടാതി (Purva Bhadrapada)
     - വ്യാഴം (Jupiter)

Every nakshatra at the same position in the cycle splits the same
way, for the same geometric reason -- this isn't three separate rules
to memorize, it's one rule (position within the 9-nakshatra block)
producing three visible patterns.

ഗണ്ഡാന്തം (Gandantham): The Three Karmic Junctions
--------------------------------------------------------

A different, more severe kind of boundary happens exactly 3 times in
the zodiac: where a **water rashi** (ജല രാശി) ends and a **fire
rashi** (അഗ്നി രാശി) begins, both a nakshatra *and* a rashi end at the
same instant -- not a split, a total severing. Following the
structural framework Dr. P.S. Nair uses for this in the Kerala
tradition:

.. list-table::
   :class: longtable
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 25 35 35

   * - Junction
     - ത്യാഗ ദോഷം (Tyaga Dosham) -- last pada
     - ഭയ ദോഷം (Bhaya Dosham) -- first pada
   * - ആയില്യം-മകം (Ashlesha-Magha)
     - ആയില്യം (Ashlesha) pada 4, end of കർക്കടകം (Karka)
     - മകം (Magha) pada 1, start of ചിങ്ങം (Simha)
   * - തൃക്കേട്ട-മൂലം (Jyeshtha-Mula)
     - തൃക്കേട്ട (Jyeshtha) pada 4, end of വൃശ്ചികം (Vrishchika)
     - മൂലം (Mula) pada 1, start of ധനു (Dhanu)
   * - രേവതി-അശ്വതി (Revati-Ashwini)
     - രേവതി (Revati) pada 4, end of മീനം (Meena)
     - അശ്വതി (Ashwini) pada 1, start of മേടം (Mesha)

Tyaga Dosham ("exhaustion") falls on the last pada of the water-sign
nakshatra -- the cycle has reached the end of its room to grow. Bhaya
Dosham ("instability") falls on the first pada of the following
fire-sign nakshatra -- a sudden, unrefined burst of energy with
nowhere yet to channel it. The Thrikketta-Moolam junction is
traditionally treated as the most severe of the three, associated
with Kula-Dosham (impact on lineage) when the Moon or Lagna falls
there.

This is exactly why the widget marks these boundaries in red on the
wheel: they're structurally distinct from the അട്ട splits above, and
a nakshatra is never both -- verified directly against the same
9-position cycle that produces the Atta patterns.

Why Two Planets Get One Sign and Five Get Two
--------------------------------------------------------

Look at the rashi-lord table in the widget and a pattern falls out
immediately: സൂര്യൻ (Sun) rules only ചിങ്ങം (Simha), and ചന്ദ്രൻ
(Moon) rules only കർക്കടകം (Karka) -- the two signs adjacent to each
other at the "top" of the zodiac. Every other classical planet --
ചൊവ്വ, ശുക്രൻ, ബുധൻ, വ്യാഴം, ശനി (Mars, Venus, Mercury, Jupiter,
Saturn) -- rules exactly two signs, arranged symmetrically outward
from that Karka-Simha pair. This is the traditional basis for calling
the Sun and Moon's own signs special, distinct from the five planets
that each govern one "day" sign and one "night" sign.

Sources & Scope
--------------------

രാശി order, നക്ഷത്രം order, the fixed 9-lord nakshatra cycle (Ketu
through Mercury, repeating three times across 27 nakshatras), and
rashi rulerships here follow the standard Parashari framework shared
across essentially every Jyotisha school, including Kerala. Malayalam
രാശി names follow the standard Malayalam-calendar terms; നക്ഷത്രം
names and lords, and the Atta/Gandantham framework, follow reference
material provided directly, cross-checked against the underlying
geometry before publishing. Rashi animal symbols follow the most
commonly cited tradition. If a specific reference text -- especially
anything Kerala-school-specific -- describes a detail differently,
that text wins; flag it and this page gets corrected.
