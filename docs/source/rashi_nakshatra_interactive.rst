Rashis, Nakshatras & Padas: Play With It
================================================

Before any interpretation, Vedic astrology is a coordinate system.
Every point in the 360-degree zodiac belongs to exactly one of 12
**rashis** (30 degrees each), one of 27 **nakshatras** (13 degrees 20
minutes each), and one of that nakshatra's 4 **padas** (3 degrees 20
minutes each) -- no ambiguity, no interpretation required, just
arithmetic on a longitude. This page is that layer only: what's
*there*, not what it *means*.

The Two Overlapping Grids
--------------------------------

Rashis and nakshatras don't share a common boundary size -- 30 degrees
doesn't divide evenly by 13 degrees 20 minutes -- so most nakshatras
straddle a rashi boundary somewhere inside them. Krittika starts in
the last few degrees of Mesha and spends its remaining three padas in
Vrishabha; Bharani, one nakshatra earlier, happens to fit entirely
inside Mesha with room to spare. Neither is an exception to a rule --
it's what falls out of two grids of different sizes laid over the
same circle. What *is* exact: 4 padas of 3 degrees 20 minutes each
always equal one nakshatra, and 9 padas always equal one rashi (30
divided by 3 degrees 20 minutes is exactly 9) -- which is exactly why
padas, not degrees, are the unit later techniques like Navamsa are
built on.

Why Two Planets Get One Sign and Five Get Two
--------------------------------------------------------

Look at the rashi-lord table in the widget and a pattern falls out
immediately: the Sun rules only Simha, and the Moon rules only Karka
-- the two signs adjacent to each other at the "top" of the zodiac.
Every other classical planet -- Mars, Venus, Mercury, Jupiter, Saturn
-- rules exactly two signs, arranged symmetrically outward from that
Karka-Simha pair. This is the traditional basis for calling the
Sun and Moon's own signs special, distinct from the five planets that
each govern one "day" sign and one "night" sign.

Sources & Scope
--------------------

The rashi order, nakshatra order, the fixed 9-lord nakshatra cycle
(Ketu through Mercury, repeating three times across 27 nakshatras --
the same cycle Vimshottari Dasha is built on), and rashi rulerships
here follow the standard Parashari framework shared across essentially
every Jyotisha school, including Kerala. Nakshatra symbol descriptions
follow the most commonly cited tradition. If a specific reference
text -- especially anything Kerala-school-specific -- describes a
detail differently, that text wins; flag it and this page gets
corrected.
